About me

I teach organisational behaviour, ethical leadership and negotiation at the Haskayne School of Business, University of Calgary. Here I share teaching experiments, practical resources and questions about how students learn.

Questions that shape my teaching

I start with questions. A case, a negotiation or a piece of literature can give us something consequential to think about together: whose perspective is missing, what information matters, and what makes a decision defensible?

I want students to practise judgement as well as understand concepts. Dialogue, practice, feedback and reflection give them ways to revisit their thinking. Clear expectations balance support and challenge, helping students make informed choices about their learning.

In ethical leadership, I have explored that balance through Specifications Grading: explicit criteria, a choice of grade bundles and opportunities to revise. The design makes requirements visible while asking students to take more responsibility for meeting them.

Where teaching meets scholarship

My background is in communication studies and political psychology. My scholarship includes leadership development, imaginative literature in leadership education, instructor humour and business case writing. Classroom experiences open larger questions about people and organisations.

Literature invites discussion about ethical choices and the perspectives through which we understand them. Cases and simulations give students decisions to work through together. These approaches prompt questions for the scholarship of teaching and learning. A promising classroom experience starts an inquiry; it does not establish what students have learned.

Keeping learners’ circumstances in view

Before my current faculty role, I worked in Student Accessibility Services and as an Academic Development Specialist at Haskayne. Those experiences keep learners’ circumstances central to my course design.

I aim to combine demanding work with useful preparation, clear expectations and opportunities to respond to feedback. Students’ questions and feedback help me see when an explanation, activity or assignment needs another look.
