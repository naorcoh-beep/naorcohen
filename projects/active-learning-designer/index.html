Active Learning Designer

An AI-powered teaching skill that turns course material or a teaching goal into a runnable learning activity, then helps instructors adapt the task, timing and facilitation.

What the Skill Can Create

MapleWorks is a fictional manufacturing exercise about the Theory of Constraints. These actual slides and simulator screenshot show an activity developed with the skill.

Rendered from the supplied PowerPoint. Open any image for a full-size view.

Read text description: MapleWorks scenario slide

MapleWorks: the production system. Each unit travels through Cutting, then Assembly, then Finishing. Cutting handles 8 units per round, Assembly 5 and Finishing 7. Demand is 7 units per round; contribution is $50 per finished unit.

Production assumptions: no defects or breakdowns; zero initial work in progress (WIP); units may pass multiple stations in the same round. Phase 1 asks the class to run two rounds in 7 minutes. These are the opening scenario assumptions; the later Round 3 simulator carries forward WIP from those rounds.

See the investment decision and simulator
Read text description: MapleWorks investment slide

Choose one improvement. Option A upgrades Cutting from 8 to 10 units per round for $40 per round. Option B adds Assembly support, increasing capacity from 5 to 7 for $60 per round. Option C upgrades Finishing from 7 to 9 for $40 per round.

The team must justify the additional finished units, additional contribution, net effect after cost and why it chose that option. Phase 3 allows 6 minutes to make the investment decision, then commit.

The interactive companion

A standalone HTML companion can make an activity’s model visible through inputs and computed outputs. This actual MapleWorks screenshot shows capacity and demand controls after the Assembly upgrade.

Read text description: MapleWorks simulator screenshot

This is a static screenshot of MapleWorks Production Flow, not an interactive simulator on this website. Four sliders show Cutting capacity 8, Assembly capacity 7, Finishing capacity 7 and customer demand 7 units per round. Each slider ranges from 1 to 12. The pictured controls include Reset original case, Reveal results, Hide results and Try Assembly upgrade (5 to 7).

After the Assembly upgrade, the displayed Round 3 results are 7 finished units, 4 units of WIP and $350 contribution at $50 per unit. Available input is 11: four opening WIP units plus seven newly cut units. The bars show available input 11, Assembly 7, Finishing 7 and demand 7; the limiting factors are Assembly, Finishing and demand.

The decision prompt compares increasing Cutting from 8 to 10, Assembly from 5 to 7, or Finishing from 7 to 9. Costs per round are $40, $60 and $40 respectively. The screenshot states that in the original case, the Assembly upgrade creates $100 extra contribution and $40 incremental benefit per round.

The model uses hypothetical deterministic figures, identical units, a fixed Cutting–Assembly–Finishing route, no losses or breakdowns, same-round transfer, and WIP carried before Assembly for the supplied Round 3 case. It is a simple capacity and flow model, not a discrete-event simulation. Throughput contribution is not accounting profit.

The model starts at Round 3 with four units of work in progress. It uses hypothetical deterministic figures and omits longer-run variability. Contribution is not accounting profit. Facilitator guidance can connect the model’s assumptions to a classroom debrief.

Fit the activity to your class

Fit the activity to your class

Bring a concept, reading, lecture segment, case or existing exercise. Specify the time, class size, delivery mode and available technology. There is no fixed activity length.

Ask for student instructions, a timed sequence, facilitator notes, a debrief and a check of understanding. You can also revise an existing activity or troubleshoot participation and discussion.

Once the design works for you, request a Classroom Delivery Package: slides, a facilitator guide and, when useful and supported, an HTML companion. File generation is optional; you can teach from the activity text.

Active Learning Designer is a prototype. Review AI suggestions against your learning goals, student needs and local requirements.
