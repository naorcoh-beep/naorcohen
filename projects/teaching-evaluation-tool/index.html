Reflective teaching workflow · In Development

Teaching Evaluation Tool

A structured AI-assisted workflow for reflecting on course evaluations, their patterns and their limits.

Read the account

The project

A public tool and downloadable package are not yet available. This page describes the project; it does not accept or process evaluation files.

The Problem

Student evaluations are supposed to help us become better teachers. Yet making sense of them is not always straightforward.

Quantitative ratings tell part of the story, while written comments often provide a different perspective. Some concerns appear repeatedly; others may be mentioned only once but raise important questions. Comparing evaluations across courses and semesters adds another layer of complexity.

Making sense of this feedback requires considering what students’ experiences might reveal about our teaching and where we could improve.

The Idea

I wanted to explore whether AI could help transform course evaluations into a more meaningful process of professional reflection.

I developed a structured analytical workflow that examines quantitative indicators, identifies themes in qualitative comments, and translates the findings into practical recommendations.

The objective was to help instructors engage more thoughtfully with the available evidence while retaining responsibility for interpreting what it means for their teaching.

What I Built

I developed a reusable Course Evaluation Analysis Skill that guides an AI assistant through the analysis of student evaluation reports.

The workflow combines quantitative analysis with qualitative interpretation, distinguishes recurring themes from isolated but potentially important concerns, and produces a Faculty Development Report.

The report identifies teaching strengths, areas requiring attention, and three evidence-based recommendations for future improvement.

Importantly, the analysis is designed to acknowledge limitations, including low response rates, changing evaluation instruments, and the difference between student perceptions and demonstrated learning.

What I Learned

A structured AI-assisted process may help us ask better questions about our teaching, as well as make analysing course evaluations more manageable.

AI can organise evidence, identify patterns, and suggest interpretations. Deciding what those patterns mean, which concerns deserve attention, and how teaching should change remains a matter of professional judgement.

The aim is to make reflection more informed, systematic, and useful.
