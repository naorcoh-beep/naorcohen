Research & writing

Leadership, learning and the ways people understand one another.

My scholarship connects teaching and educational practice with a background in communication studies and political psychology. Current interests include leadership development, imaginative literature in leadership education, instructor humour and business case writing.

Explore journal articles, my dissertation and thesis, teaching cases and other writing. Links lead to publisher or institutional records; full texts and teaching notes are not hosted here.

Journal articles

  • No Funny Business: Instructor Humour as Pedagogical Leadership in Transformational Teaching (opens in a new tab)

    Cohen, N.; Alexander, S. M.; Powell, D.; Turner, N.

    2026 · Active Learning in Higher Education

    The study examines how course-related and self-disparaging instructor humour relate to students’ perceptions of transformational teaching. It also considers whether these relationships differ according to students’ educational orientations.

  • On the Duty to Host in International Law: Jacques Derrida and Political Literature (opens in a new tab)

    Cohen, N.; Fuchs, I.

    2022 · Law, Culture and the Humanities · 18(1) · 114–131

    First published online 2017.

    Reading J. M. Coetzee’s Disgrace through Derrida’s account of hospitality, this article examines tensions between hosts and guests in debates about refugees and displacement. It asks whether liberal democracies and international humanitarian law can support hospitality that is both ethically grounded and workable in practice.

  • What Does National Resilience Mean in a Democracy? Evidence from the United States and Israel (opens in a new tab)

    Canetti, D.; Waismel-Manor, I.; Cohen, N.; Rapaport, C.

    2014 · Armed Forces & Society · 40(3) · 504–520

    First published online 2013.

    Comparing citizens’ definitions in the United States and Israel, this study examines the political and psychological foundations of national resilience. Perceived threats, optimism, patriotism and trust in government emerge as recurring components, informing a revised understanding of the concept.

    Jyoti, D.; Wall, T.; Jolliffe, P.; Cohen, N.; Lewis, M.

    2026 · Chartered Association of Business Schools · Commentary
